# Policy Model

## 1. Objectives

The policy model aims to:

- define how AI-generated surplus is collected,
- describe how it is governed,
- and specify how it is distributed as a dividend.

It is not a full legal framework.  
It is a **conceptual scaffold** for future policy work.

## 2. Three-Layer Structure

### 2.1. Collection Layer

Sources of AI surplus:

- **AI profit share**
  - Dedicated tax or profit share on:
    - frontier AI companies,
    - large-scale AI deployments.
  - Includes:
    - windfall clauses (higher share at extreme profit levels),
    - baseline ongoing contributions.

- **Compute utility surplus**
  - Public or cooperative ownership of compute infrastructure.
  - Compute priced above marginal cost.
  - Surplus routed into the dividend fund.

- **Data dividends**
  - Payments for access to large-scale public data pools.
  - Licensing fees from model developers.
  - Treated as shared resource revenue.

### 2.2. Governance Layer

Managed by an **AI Dividend Authority** (ADA), with:

- transparent reporting,
- algorithmic rules,
- democratic oversight.

Key functions:

- set and adjust τ (public share of AI rents),
- manage reserves,
- publish scenario analyses,
- coordinate with other institutions (e.g., central banks, treasuries).

### 2.3. Distribution Layer

Outputs:

- **Universal cash dividend**
  - Monthly or quarterly payments.
  - Equal baseline per person.

- **Optional targeted adjustments**
  - Additional support for:
    - displaced workers,
    - regions heavily impacted by automation.

- **Infrastructure credits**
  - Non-cash credits for:
    - compute,
    - agents,
    - connectivity,
    - education.

This aligns with the goal of **egalitarian access to infrastructure**, not just money.

## 3. Core Policy Parameter: τ



\[
D = \frac{\tau R}{N}
\]



Where:

- \(R\): total AI-generated returns,
- \(N\): population,
- \(\tau\): share of AI rents allocated to the dividend.

Policy questions:

- What is the “right” τ?
- How does τ change over time?
- How does τ interact with:
  - other taxes,
  - public spending,
  - private investment?

The simulator exists to make these questions **quantitative and legible**.

## 4. Relationship to Existing Ideas

This model intersects with:

- universal basic income (UBI),
- sovereign wealth funds,
- resource dividends (e.g., Alaska),
- data trusts,
- and public utilities.

The difference:

- It is explicitly tied to **AI-generated surplus**, not general taxation.
- It treats AI as **infrastructure built on public input**, not just private innovation.

## 5. Role of Citizens and Agents

Citizens:

- receive dividends,
- gain access to infrastructure,
- and can use personal agents to:
  - understand policy,
  - simulate futures,
  - allocate resources.

Agents:

- become tools for:
  - civic understanding,
  - economic planning,
  - personal empowerment.

Policy is no longer opaque.  
It becomes something people can **interact with**, not just endure.
